This batch of blog posts is a bit of a mixed bag, but there's a common theme running through all of them: **making technology work the way I want it to, rather than the other way around.**

From building a new home for my website to automating newsletters, simplifying calendar workflows, and finding increasingly ridiculous ways to deal with spam, I've been spending a lot of time tinkering with the tools I already use.

And, as usual, Apple Shortcuts and Markdown have found their way into just about everything.

## A New Home on the Internet

I've given my little corner of the internet an upgrade.

In [A New Landing Page & the Blog's New Home](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/a-new-landing-page--the-blogs-new-home), I walk through building a new landing page at [aidanmaurinjones.com](https://aidanmaurinjones.com/) using Hugo and the Hermit-V2 theme.

The idea was simple: create a proper home for everything I do online, while giving the blog its own dedicated space.

Nothing overly complicated, nothing bloated, and certainly no need for an expensive website builder. Just a lightweight website that I can maintain myself.

## Why I Still Haven't Fallen in Love with Obsidian

Apparently, I'm supposed to love Obsidian.

It's powerful, customizable, Markdown-based, and checks just about every box that should make it perfect for me.

Except it isn't.

In [Why Obsidian Doesn't Work for Me](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/why-obsidian-doesnt-work-for-me), I explain why I keep returning to BBEdit, Marked 2, and the tools I've already built my workflow around.

Sometimes the best application isn't the one with the most features. It's the one that gets out of your way.

And for me, that apparently isn't Obsidian.

## One Markdown File, Two Destinations

This might be one of my favourite little workflow improvements.

In [Automating My Newsletter Workflow: One Markdown File for Hugo and Buttondown](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/automating-my-newsletter-workflow-one-markdown-file-for-hugo-and-buttondown), I explain how I built an Apple Shortcut that takes a single Markdown file and publishes it to both my Hugo newsletter archive and Buttondown.

No copying and pasting between editors. No maintaining two versions of the same newsletter. No unnecessary extra steps.

I write once, run a Shortcut, and let the automation handle the rest.

It's a relatively small improvement, but those are often the ones I appreciate the most.

## Fighting Spam with Questionable Humour

Not every automation needs to solve a serious problem.

Sometimes it's enough to make you laugh.

In [Fighting Spam with Automation: A Shortcut for Writing Hilarious Replies](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/fighting-spam-with-automation--a-shortcut-for-writing-hilarious-replies), I combine AppleScript, Apple Shortcuts, and LM Studio to automatically draft absurdly rude responses to spam emails.

Is this necessary? Absolutely not.

Was it fun to build? Absolutely.

And frankly, if someone is going to fill my inbox with garbage, I might as well get some entertainment out of it.

## Two Small Shortcuts That Make Life Easier

I've also published a couple of smaller, more practical Shortcuts.

[Get This Week's Events: A Smarter Calendar Shortcut](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/get-this-weeks-events-a-smarter-calendar-shortcut) covers a flexible way to calculate the beginning and end of the current week automatically, making it easy to retrieve just the calendar events that matter.

Meanwhile, [Convert Current Date to Unix Time with Shortcuts](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/convert-current-date-to-unix-time-with-shortcuts) demonstrates a simple way to convert dates into Unix timestamps.

Neither is particularly flashy, but both are useful building blocks for larger automations.

And that's something I really enjoy about Shortcuts. A handful of relatively simple actions can become the foundation for something much more interesting.

## The Bigger Picture

Looking at these projects together, I think they demonstrate something I've come to appreciate more and more: **good workflows don't necessarily require complicated software.**

My website runs on Hugo. My writing lives in Markdown files. BBEdit and Marked 2 handle editing and previewing. Apple Shortcuts connects everything together.

Even my ridiculous spam-reply generator fits into that philosophy.

I'm not particularly interested in replacing my entire workflow every time a new application becomes popular. I'd rather take the tools I already enjoy using and find clever ways to make them work together.

Sometimes that means building a website. Sometimes it means automating a newsletter. And sometimes it means teaching a local language model to insult spammers.

That's the fun of tinkering.
