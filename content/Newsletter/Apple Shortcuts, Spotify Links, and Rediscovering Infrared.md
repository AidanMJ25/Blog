This week's posts are a mixture of Apple Shortcuts, fixing minor annoyances, and digging through the surprisingly extensive world of infrared remote controls.

As usual, the common theme is finding ways to make technology do what I want, rather than working around its limitations.

### Fixing Disappearing Lock Screen Widgets with Apple Shortcuts

For whatever reason, iOS has developed an annoying habit of making my Lock Screen widgets disappear whenever their corresponding applications are updated.

Rather than repeatedly opening each application to restore its widget, I built a simple Apple Shortcut that does the job for me.

It's a small automation, but one that has worked reliably every time I've needed it.

[Read: Fixing Disappearing Lock Screen Widgets with Apple Shortcuts](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/fixing-disappearing-lock-screen-widgets-with-apple-shortcuts/)

### Converting Spotify Links with Apple Shortcuts

I use YouTube Music, which makes receiving Spotify links slightly inconvenient.

Instead of manually searching for every song someone sends me, I built a Shortcut that uses Song.link's API to retrieve the song title and artist from a Spotify URL, then opens a corresponding YouTube search.

No Spotify account required, and no need to copy and paste song titles between applications.

[Read: Converting Spotify Links with Apple Shortcuts](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/converting-spotify-links-with-apple-shortcuts/)

### Rediscovering Infrared -- From Harmony to HAIR

Infrared remote controls might seem like yesterday's technology, but they're still incredibly useful, particularly when you want to control devices locally without relying on manufacturer applications or cloud services.

This post explores several open-source infrared code databases, including the enormous archive of Logitech Harmony remote codes, and how projects like HAIR can make those codes useful again through Home Assistant and ESPHome.

It turns out there's a remarkable amount of useful information hiding in old remote-control databases.

[Read: Rediscovering Infrared -- From Harmony to HAIR](https://blog.aidanmaurinjones.com/posts/rediscovering-infrared---from-harmony-to-hair/)
* * *

### A Few Closing Thoughts

These three posts cover rather different subjects, but they all reflect something I enjoy about tinkering with technology: finding practical solutions to problems that probably shouldn't exist in the first place.

Sometimes that's a Shortcut that saves a few taps. Other times it's digging through decades of infrared codes to make an old piece of hardware useful again.

Not every project needs to be particularly complicated or revolutionary. Sometimes making something work a little better is reason enough to build it.
